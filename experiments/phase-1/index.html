MARCmin · Experiment 1 · Preliminary · November 2026

Does readable MARC help small models find books?

A first test on 3,781 real Library of Congress records and 120 questions. In this run, rewriting records as plain labeled text took small search models from missing half or more of the books to finding nearly all of them.

Preliminary. The questions were drafted by an AI assistant while reading the records, and no cataloger has reviewed them yet. They are probably too easy. The two larger models and the Library of Congress benchmark have not run yet. Treat the direction as a signal and the size as unknown.

Why raw records fail

Length is the likely culprit. A raw Library of Congress record averages about 654 tokens, the word pieces models read. Pre-encoded, the same record is about 127; the compact template is about 56. These small models read only the first 256 or 512 tokens and drop the rest.

Raw records open with administrative fields, such as acquisition notes and batch-update logs. The subject headings, the most useful part for search, come near the end and are often cut off.

What was tested

Records. 3,781 English-language books with subject headings, drawn at random from five Library of Congress classes: science (953), medicine (1000), agriculture (860), geography and recreation (449) and fine arts (519). They come from the Library's embedded-catalog files, with full records fetched from its public search service.

Questions. 120 short, patron-style questions, 24 per class. Each is tied to the one record that should answer it. Examples:

  • "kids book explaining how submarines sink and float"
    Science · expects: Dive! dive!
  • "treating anorexia, bulimia and obesity in kids and teenagers"
    Medicine · expects: Evaluation and management of eating disorders: anorexia, bulimia, and obesity
  • "what to feed honeybees and when"
    Agriculture · expects: Feeding bees
  • "trail guide to day hikes and backpacking trips in Nevada"
    Geography and recreation · expects: Hiking Nevada: a guide to the state's greatest hiking adventures
  • "Boucher's designs for the Beauvais tapestry works"
    Fine arts · expects: François Boucher and the Beauvais tapestries

Record formats. Raw MARC as catalogers see it; fully pre-encoded, with labels and codes spelled out; the compact template from Chapter 2; and an approximation of the Library of Congress's descriptive-fields input, whose exact format is not published.

Methods. Keyword search (BM25) and two small open embedding models, MiniLM (22M parameters) and BGE-small (33M), run on an ordinary CPU.

Measure. Where the expected record ranks among all 3,781. Other relevant records were not judged, so some "misses" may be reasonable answers.

What this does not show yet

How big the gap really is. Pre-encoded formats hit the ceiling: 99 to 100% in the top 10. That says the questions are too easy, and a ceiling hides real differences.

Which format is best. Full, compact and descriptive-fields-only score within a few points of each other. A harder question set is needed to separate them.

Whether bigger models close the gap. The model that reads 512 tokens did better on raw MARC than the one that reads 256. Larger models may narrow the gap further; they have not run yet.

What happens next

Needs catalogers

Review the questions

Check each question against its expected record, mark better answers, and rewrite questions that are too easy. A spreadsheet is ready, with a catalog link for every record.

Download the 120 questions (CSV)

Needs Amazon Bedrock access

Score the Library of Congress benchmark

The Library of Congress embedded its catalog with Amazon's Titan model, so the questions must go through the same model. The kit includes the script. It costs under a cent.

About the benchmark

Running elsewhere

Larger models

BGE-base (110M) and BGE-large (335M) need more memory than the first machine had. The full kit runs on any computer with Python and a few gigabytes free.
